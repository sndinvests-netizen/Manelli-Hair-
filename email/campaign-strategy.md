# Manelli Hair — "The Return" Email Campaign Strategy
### Repeat Purchase Campaign | Existing Customers

---

## Campaign Overview

**Campaign Name:** The Return

**Objective:** Drive repeat purchases from existing customers by leveraging identity, lifecycle timing, and community belonging — not discounts.

**Core Insight:** The repeat purchase journey for a $310–$425 product is emotional, not transactional. These women bought Manelli the first time because it matched who they are. This campaign reminds them that who they are keeps evolving — and so should their hair.

---

## Sequence Structure

| Email | Name | Send Day | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Check-In | Day 1 | Re-engage, warm relationship, plant desire (no offer) |
| 2 | The Upgrade | Day 7 | Drive consideration, introduce VIP loyalty reward |
| 3 | Last Call | Day 10 (openers only) | Convert with dignified urgency, hard close |

---

## Discount Code Strategy

The governing principle: **A Manelli discount is never a price reduction. It is a private loyalty recognition.**

### Codes by Segment

| Code | Value | Audience | Framing |
|---|---|---|---|
| `THERETURN` | 10% off + free shipping | Loyalists (0–6 months post-purchase) | "Because loyalty at this level looks different" |
| `ICONIC15` | 15% off | Window Shoppers (6–9 months, no repeat) | "Your VIP loyalty code — reserved for Manelli women" |
| `PRIVATE15` | 15% off | Gifted segment | "Your private client access code" |
| `LEGACY20` | 20% off | Lapsed (9–18 months, no repeat) | "A welcome back on your own terms" |
| No code | Early access / personal note | Multi-Purchase VIPs (2+ purchases) | Treat as insiders, not discount recipients |

### Language Rules
- **Never say:** discount, sale, deal, save, limited time offer
- **Always say:** private access, client recognition, reserved for you, not available to the public, loyalty reward
- Codes expire after 7 days — language: "Your access closes [Date]" — never "Sale ends Sunday!"
- No countdown timers — date-specific language in clean typography only

### Luxury Urgency Tactics
1. **Access expiration, not sale end** — "Your private access closes [Date]."
2. **Capacity language** — "This recognition is extended to a limited number of clients."
3. **The quiet close** — One calm, final line in Email 3: "After [Date], this access will not be re-extended."
4. **Time of day** — Send final close at 10am (Tuesday/Wednesday). Never midnight — it reads as panic.
5. **No countdown timers** — Countdown timers are a fast-fashion tactic.

## Psychological Triggers by Email

### Email 1 — Identity Continuity + Nostalgic Activation
- **Identity Continuity:** Name her original product, tie it to her identity ("You chose The CEO for a reason"). Activates the need to stay consistent with her self-image.
- **Nostalgic Activation:** Use sensory language that triggers the memory of her first unboxing — without being literal.
- **No-pressure priming:** Show new products without asking to buy. Lowers resistance for Email 2's ask.

### Email 2 — Reciprocity + Exclusivity + Status Affirmation
- **Reciprocity:** The brand gives first (private access, curated picks, VIP code). The psychological pull to reciprocate via purchase is strong.
- **Exclusivity:** "This is not public" converts a promotion into a privilege. Desire escalates.
- **Status Affirmation:** Buying again signals she is a woman who does not compromise.

### Email 3 — Loss Aversion + Dignified Scarcity
- **Loss Aversion:** "Your access closes Friday" is more powerful than "Shop now and save."
- **Dignified Scarcity:** The door is closing because Manelli moves forward — not because she failed to act.
- **Zero desperation:** If the email reads needy, it collapses brand positioning built in Emails 1 and 2.

## Segmentation Strategy

### Segment 1 — The Loyalists
- **Definition:** Purchased within the last 1–6 months
- **Goal:** Reinforce loyalty, introduce complementary products
- **Code:** THERETURN (10% + free shipping)
- **Tone:** Warmest, most intimate — she is already in the Manelli world
- **Product focus:** Products adjacent to her purchase (e.g., if she bought The CEO → suggest The HBIC or The Main Character)

### Segment 2 — The Window Shoppers *(highest ROI target)*
- **Definition:** Purchased once, 6–9 months ago, no repeat
- **Goal:** Re-engage and convert to second purchase before 12-month lapse
- **Code:** ICONIC15 (15% off)
- **Tone:** Aspirational and forward-looking — position repurchase as natural evolution
- **Product focus:** Her original product + one new character she hasn't seen

### Segment 3 — The Lapsed (High-Value Winback)
- **Definition:** Purchased 9–18 months ago, no repeat
- **Goal:** Bring them back without conceding brand equity
- **Code:** LEGACY20 (20% off)
- **Tone:** Confident and welcoming, never apologetic. Never "we've missed you." Say: "Your standard is still here."
- **Product focus:** Most aspirational products — The HBIC, The Main Character, The Iconic Blonde
- **Consider:** Small gift-with-purchase to increase perceived value without raising the discount %

### Segment 4 — The Multi-Purchase VIPs
- **Definition:** Purchased 2+ times
- **Goal:** Make them feel categorically different. Deepen brand affinity.
- **Reward:** No discount — early access to new drops, handwritten note, private first-look
- **Tone:** The most exclusive of any segment. These are not customers — they are Manelli women.
- **Product focus:** New arrivals, anything not yet in the main catalog

### Segment 5 — The Gifted
- **Definition:** Original purchase was a gift (flag by shipping vs. billing address divergence)
- **Goal:** Convert the gift recipient into a self-purchaser
- **Code:** PRIVATE15 (15% off)
- **Tone:** Introduce Manelli as if for the first time but with warmth. "You wore Manelli. Now it's time to choose it for yourself."
- **Product focus:** Entry-point product ($310) plus aspirational upsell

## Brand Voice Non-Negotiables

1. Never use the word "discount" — use: private access, recognition, loyalty reward, client exclusive
2. Never apologize for the price point
3. No exclamation points in subject lines — they belong to fast fashion
4. Every email readable in under 60 seconds — luxury does not overstay its welcome
5. CTAs: "Claim" and "Discover" outperform "Shop" and "Buy Now" for this audience
6. Images: show the woman wearing the product, not the product alone — identity-first always
